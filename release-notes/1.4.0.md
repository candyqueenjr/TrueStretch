## TrueStretch 1.4.0 — See how your last match really ran

### What's new
- **Optimize Max now shows your real FPS from your last match.** At the end of every round, VALORANT writes its own performance statistics into its log: how many frames it drew, how long the round took, and how long the graphics card worked on each frame. TrueStretch now reads them and shows one row under "Your PC", for example "Your last match: 241 FPS average".
- **It tells you what is holding your FPS back.** If the graphics card was busy almost the whole frame, the graphics card is the limit, and lower graphics quality or a stretched resolution gives you the most extra frames. If it was mostly waiting, your processor is the limit, so lowering graphics quality adds little and the Windows, startup-app and background-program checks matter most. If you use the in-game FPS limit and reached it, the row says that instead.
- **It points out stutters and memory problems.** If more than 1% of frames were slower than 60 FPS, if Windows memory was over 90% full during the match, or if the game had shader stutters, the row says so in plain words.

### Why it matters for the player
Most "FPS boost" advice is guesswork. This row uses VALORANT's own measurement of your own match, so you know whether changing graphics settings will actually help on your PC, or whether the fix is somewhere else.

### Details
- Read-only: TrueStretch only reads VALORANT's log files (the current one and, right after the game starts, the previous session's). Nothing in the game is touched.
- If you haven't played a match yet, the row doesn't appear.
- Also checked for this release: VALORANT 13.10 is now live on our test PC and every game-log line TrueStretch relies on for stretching still looks the same.
