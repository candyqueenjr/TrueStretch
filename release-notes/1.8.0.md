## TrueStretch 1.8.0 — See the delay the game itself adds to your aim

### What's new
- **Game delay in your last match.** The "Your last match" row (under Your PC) now also shows how many milliseconds VALORANT itself took per frame on average, for example "Game delay: 6.1 ms per frame on average". This is the part of your input delay that the game adds (VALORANT calls it "engine latency"). Your mouse and monitor add their own delay on top.
- **Before vs after Optimize Max, now for delay too.** When TrueStretch can compare your matches before and after your first Optimize Max change, it now shows the game delay for both sides as well as the FPS, for example "Game delay since Optimize Max: 5.8 ms, before: 6.4 ms."
- The numbers come from VALORANT's own statistics, which the game writes into its log at the end of every round. TrueStretch only reads them. Nothing is changed and nothing is sent anywhere.

### Why it matters for the player
FPS is only half of how a game feels. The other half is how quickly your mouse movement turns into a picture. Now you can see the game's own share of that delay on your PC, and whether it went down after Optimize Max. More FPS usually means less delay.

### Details
- The delay is averaged over every frame of the match, so long rounds count more than short ones.
- Older game logs without this line simply don't show it. Everything else works as before.
- Also checked for this release: VALORANT 13.10. Every game-log line TrueStretch relies on still looks the same.
