## TrueStretch 1.9.0 — When your processor limits FPS, see who is using it

### What's new
- **Who took the processor.** When your processor (not the graphics card) sets your FPS, the "Your last match" row (under Your PC) now also says how the processor was shared during the match, for example "Of your 16 processor threads, VALORANT itself kept about 4.2 busy on average and your other programs about 2.7." It then tells you what that means: if your other programs took a whole thread or more, that is processor time taken from the game, and the Startup and Background checks show what to close. If they took little, the processor's own speed is the limit, and the power settings keep it at full speed.
- The numbers come from VALORANT's own statistics, which the game writes into its log at the end of every round. TrueStretch only reads them. Nothing is changed and nothing is sent anywhere.

### Why it matters for the player
On many PCs VALORANT is limited by the processor, and "close your background programs" is advice everyone gives without knowing whether it applies to you. Now you can see it: how much of your processor the game used and how much everything else used while you played.

### Details
- Shown only when the processor sets the FPS (or processor and graphics card both do). When the graphics card or your in-game FPS limit sets the FPS, the row stays as it was.
- Averaged over the match, weighted by round length.
- Older game logs without these lines simply don't show it. Everything else works as before.
- Also checked for this release: VALORANT 13.10. Every game-log line TrueStretch relies on still looks the same.
