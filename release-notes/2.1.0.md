## TrueStretch 2.1.0 — Servers: your ping to every VALORANT server, and Riot's status for your region

A new **Servers** tab on the VALORANT page. VALORANT measures its ping to every game server of your region each
time it starts; until now that number stayed hidden in the game's log. TrueStretch now reads it, keeps it (VALORANT
throws its old logs away after a few sessions), and tells you what it means.

### What's new
- **Ping board.** Every server of your region, lowest ping first, with a bar for the ping at your last game start
  and a thin mark for your typical ping over the last two weeks. The server VALORANT prefers for you is tagged
  **YOUR SERVER**, the lowest one **LOWEST**. Hover a row for best and worst.
- **Three numbers at a glance.** *Your server* (VALORANT's pick and whether that ping is great, good, okay or
  high), *Lowest ping*, and *Between game starts* (how much your ping moved from one game start to the next:
  steady or unsteady).
- **What this means**, in plain words. If VALORANT prefers a server that isn't your lowest one, it says so (there is
  no server picker in VALORANT — matchmaking decides). If your ping is steady, it tells you to keep Network
  Buffering on Minimum; if it jumps between sessions, it suggests Moderate and a cable instead of Wi-Fi.
- **Riot server status for your region.** Current incidents and maintenance for VALORANT on Windows, straight from
  Riot's public status feed, with a link to the status page. "All good" when there's nothing — and months-old
  notices Riot never took down are counted, not shown.
- **Nothing to set up.** The board fills in by itself after the game has started once. Your account ID, which sits
  on the same log line, is never kept. Nothing is sent anywhere; the only connection is to Riot's status feed.

### What's better
- The summary cards on the Matches page and the new Servers tab share one design.
- Example data in the screenshots is clearly marked as such in the tests and the website scripts.
