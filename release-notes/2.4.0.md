## TrueStretch 2.4.0 — Optimize Max tells you what it did, and every change stays undoable

Optimize Max day. Until now, pressing **Optimize** made the fixed rows quietly disappear from the list and showed a
message for a few seconds — easy to miss. And *Undo all* was one click on the main card with no way back.
This update makes it clear what changed, keeps every change in one place, and makes Undo work in more situations.

### What's new
- **A result card after Optimize.** It stays at the top of the list until you dismiss it: how many changes were made
  and checked, what they improve (for example *Lower input delay · 2*, *More FPS · 1*), which settings changed, and
  for anything that didn't work, the reason in plain words. On a small window the page scrolls to it by itself.
- **Your changes.** A new button on the score card (*Your changes (5)*) opens a list of everything TrueStretch changed
  on your PC, newest first: what it was before and after (*Off → On*), when it changed, and an **Undo** button for
  each one. *Undo all* lives there now and asks for a second click before it does anything.
- **Restart reminder.** Some changes (like GPU scheduling) only take effect after a restart. The score card now
  says *Restart your PC to finish: …* and keeps saying it until Windows has actually restarted — not just for a few
  seconds after you press Optimize.
- **New check: Windows Update uploading to other PCs.** Windows can share updates it downloaded with strangers'
  PCs on the internet, which uses your upload speed in the background and can cause ping spikes. If yours is set to
  *PCs on the internet*, Optimize Max switches it to *PCs on my local network* (your own PCs still share updates).
  If your school or company set it, TrueStretch leaves it alone.

### What's better
- Turning mouse acceleration back on with Undo takes effect straight away, also from the saved value.
- *Undo all* is no longer a one-click button on the main card; it's in *Your changes* and asks first.

### What's fixed
- **Undo works even when the setting isn't listed any more.** Every change now saves the exact value from before in
  TrueStretch's undo file. Before, Undo needed today's scan to show the same setting — if you uninstalled VALORANT,
  switched power plans or removed a startup program, that change could never be undone. Now it can.
